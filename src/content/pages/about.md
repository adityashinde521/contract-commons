## Overview

<div class="goal"><b>Goal:</b> you know what the course covers, which track fits you and what you will be able to do at the end.</div>
    <h4>Why this course</h4>
    <p>A smart contract is a program that holds and moves value by itself. It changes how agreements are made, performed and enforced. To use it well you need three views at once: how the code works (computer science), why a process should be automated and what it costs (business informatics), and what the code means legally (law). This course puts the three views in one room and one team.</p>
    <h4>How the course is built</h4>
    <div class="tw"><table>
      <tr><th>Section</th><th>What you do</th><th>Who</th></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Theory</b> T1–T10</td><td>Short notes, videos and a quiz for each idea: ledger, hashes, keys, consensus, Ethereum, smart contracts, Solidity, security, tokens, law and business.</td><td>Everyone. Law students can skip T7–T9.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Remix labs</b> L0–L7</td><td>Write, deploy and test eight contracts in the browser. End on the public Sepolia testnet.</td><td>CS students at the keyboard. BI and law students in the team.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>GUI tool labs</b> G0–G12</td><td>Use real market tools: build contracts with OpenZeppelin and thirdweb, run a Safe multisig and an Aragon DAO, notarise with EAS, draft with the Accord Project, then debug with Tenderly, trace hacks with Phalcon and research with Dune and L2BEAT. No programming.</td><td>Law and BI students. Recommended for CS too.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Frontiers</b> F1–F7</td><td>Current topics: rollups, zero-knowledge, AI agents with wallets, quantum risk, recent hacks, tokenisation and regulation, research.</td><td>Everyone.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Project</b> P1–P3</td><td>Capstone in mixed teams, team lab formats and research topics.</td><td>Everyone, in teams of 3–4.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Final exam</b></td><td>20 random questions from all quizzes. Pass with 80%.</td><td>Everyone.</td></tr>
    </table></div>

## Learning outcomes

<p>At the end of the course you can:</p>
    <div class="aud">
      <div class="cs"><h5>Computer science</h5><ul>
        <li>Explain hashes, signatures, consensus, gas and the EVM.</li>
        <li>Write, test and deploy Solidity contracts with state machines, access control, time rules and events.</li>
        <li>Find and fix reentrancy and access-control bugs.</li>
        <li>Deploy and verify contracts on a public testnet.</li>
      </ul></div>
      <div class="bi"><h5>Business informatics</h5><ul>
        <li>Model a business process as a state machine.</li>
        <li>Decide when a blockchain is the right tool and when a database is better.</li>
        <li>Estimate transaction costs and compare them with an intermediary.</li>
        <li>Describe token-based business models and their risks.</li>
      </ul></div>
      <div class="lw"><h5>Law</h5><ul>
        <li>Distinguish a smart contract, a legal contract and a smart legal contract.</li>
        <li>Create on-chain records and contracts with no-code tools and explain what they prove.</li>
        <li>Analyse identity, mandatory law, GDPR, jurisdiction and liability problems in concrete contracts.</li>
        <li>Classify tokens under MiCA and MiFID II at an introductory level.</li>
      </ul></div>
    </div>
    <h4>Shared outcome</h4>
    <p>All students can take one contract (for example the Escrow) and explain it in all three languages: as code, as a process and as a legal relationship.</p>
