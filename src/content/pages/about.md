## Overview

<div class="goal"><b>Goal:</b> you know what the course covers, what every student does and what you need to pass.</div>
    <h4>Why this course</h4>
    <p>A smart contract is a program that holds and moves value by itself. It changes how agreements are made, performed and enforced. To use it well you need three views at once: how the code works (technology), why a process should be automated and what it costs (business), and what the code means legally (law). In this course every student learns all three. There is one path, one set of labs and one final exam for everyone.</p>
    <h4>How the course is built</h4>
    <div class="tw"><table>
      <tr><th>Part</th><th>What you do</th></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Lessons</b></td><td>Short notes, videos and a quiz for each idea: ledgers, hashes, keys, consensus, Ethereum, Solidity, tokens, security, scaling, DeFi, DAOs, law and regulation.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Code labs</b></td><td>Read, deploy and change real contracts in Remix in the browser, then on the public Sepolia testnet. You change and test logic; you do not write programs from scratch.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Tool labs</b></td><td>Use real market tools: OpenZeppelin Wizard, thirdweb, Safe, Aragon, EAS, the Accord Project, Tenderly, Phalcon, Dune and L2BEAT.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Three lenses</b></td><td>Every lesson ends with the same contract seen through technology, business and law. You answer all three.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Capstone</b></td><td>A team project: contract, process model and legal text for one system.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><b>Final exam</b></td><td>The same 40-question exam for everyone. Pass with 70%.</td></tr>
    </table></div>
    <h4>What you need to pass</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Every lesson and lab completed: each quiz passed with 80%, each lab logged.</li>
      <li>The capstone, presented by the team. Every member can explain every part.</li>
      <li>The final exam: 40 questions, 70% to pass. No code writing. You fill in missing logic, fix a wrong line, put steps in order and answer concept questions.</li>
    </ul>

## Learning outcomes

<p>At the end of the course every student can:</p>
    <div class="aud">
      <div class="cs"><h5>Technology lens</h5><ul>
        <li>Explain hashes, signatures, consensus, gas and the EVM.</li>
        <li>Read a Solidity contract and say what each function allows and forbids.</li>
        <li>Complete missing logic and fix a wrong line: a wrong check, a wrong order, a wrong address or unit.</li>
        <li>Deploy and verify a contract on a public testnet.</li>
      </ul></div>
      <div class="bi"><h5>Business lens</h5><ul>
        <li>Model a business process as a state machine.</li>
        <li>Decide when a blockchain is the right tool and when a database is better.</li>
        <li>Estimate transaction costs and compare them with an intermediary.</li>
        <li>Describe token-based business models and their risks.</li>
      </ul></div>
      <div class="lw"><h5>Law lens</h5><ul>
        <li>Distinguish a smart contract, a legal contract and a smart legal contract.</li>
        <li>Create on-chain records with market tools and explain what they prove.</li>
        <li>Analyse identity, mandatory law, GDPR, jurisdiction and liability problems in concrete contracts.</li>
        <li>Classify tokens under MiCA and MiFID II at an introductory level.</li>
      </ul></div>
    </div>
    <h4>Shared outcome</h4>
    <p>Each student can take one contract (for example the Escrow) and explain it in all three lenses: as code, as a process and as a legal relationship.</p>
